# German A1: Adjective Declension
# Adjektivdeklination

## 1. What is adjective declension?

**Adjective declension** means changing the ending of an adjective when it comes before a noun.

Compare:

- Die Frau ist **nett**. — The woman is nice.
- Das ist die **nette Frau**. — This is the nice woman.

The adjective *nett* changes to *nette* because it comes before the noun *Frau* and follows an article.

### The basic rule

| Position | Example | Adjective |
|---|---|---|
| After *sein* (to be) | Die Frau ist nett. | nett |
| Before a noun | die nette Frau | nette |

Adjectives after **sein**, **werden**, and **bleiben** generally do not take endings.

Examples:
- Das Haus ist **groß**. — The house is big.
- Das **große Haus** ist schön. — The big house is beautiful.

For now, remember: adjectives directly before nouns usually need an ending.

---

## 2. Adjective endings with der, die, and das

Let's begin with the **Nominativ** (the case commonly used for the subject of a sentence).

When an adjective comes before a noun with a definite article (*der, die, das*), the adjective usually takes **-e** in the singular and **-en** in the plural.

| Gender | Article + adjective + noun | English |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | der nett**e** Mann | the nice man |
| Feminine | die nett**e** Frau | the nice woman |
| Neuter | das nett**e** Kind | the nice child |
| Plural | die nett**en** Kinder | the nice children |

Notice:
- der → der nett**e** Mann
- die → die nett**e** Frau
- das → das nett**e** Kind
- die (plural) → die nett**en** Kinder

So, in the Nominativ, the adjective ending is **-e** for singular nouns and **-en** for plural nouns when used with these definite articles.

Your example:
- **Die nette Frau** = the nice woman.
- **Grace ist nett.** = Grace is nice.
- **Grace ist die nette Frau.** = Grace is the nice woman.

---

## 3. What about -er, -e, and -es?

These endings often appear with **indefinite articles** (*ein, eine*) in the Nominativ.

| Gender | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | ein nett**er** Mann | a nice man |
| Feminine | eine nett**e** Frau | a nice woman |
| Neuter | ein nett**es** Kind | a nice child |

For these examples, remember:
- Masculine: **-er**
- Feminine: **-e**
- Neuter: **-es**

Why do the endings differ? The word *ein* does not clearly show the masculine or neuter ending in the same way *der* and *das* do, so the adjective helps show the gender.

- ein nett**er** Mann
- ein nett**es** Kind

The feminine article *eine* clearly indicates feminine gender, so the adjective takes **-e**:
- eine nett**e** Frau

---

## 4. Definite vs. indefinite articles

| Gender | Definite article | Indefinite article |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | der nett**e** Mann | ein nett**er** Mann |
| Feminine | die nett**e** Frau | eine nett**e** Frau |
| Neuter | das nett**e** Kind | ein nett**es** Kind |
| Plural | die nett**en** Kinder | keine nett**en** Kinder |

Remember:
- **Definite articles:** der, die, das
- **Indefinite articles:** ein, eine
- **Negative article:** kein, keine

There is no plural form of *ein/eine*. To say “no children,” for example, use **keine Kinder**.

---

## 5. A simple memory table

For now, focus only on the Nominativ.

| Article | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **der / die / das** | der nett**e** Mann | die nett**e** Frau | das nett**e** Kind |
| **ein / eine** | ein nett**er** Mann | eine nett**e** Frau | ein nett**es** Kind |

You do not need to learn all adjective endings at once. Start with the Nominativ, then learn Akkusativ and Dativ later.
